Leveraging Sports Identities for Future Performance & Well-Being

A collaboration to help students grow and perform as ethical, mindful business leaders, with their sports identities as the through line.

Business students are working through real psychological hurdles on the way to growing and performing as ethical business leaders, and they carry valuable but dormant sports identities. Sport is a performance environment they already understand: an ideal conduit for the inner and outer exploration at the heart of Gonzaga’s Jesuit mission, drawing on self-awareness, discernment, and cura personalis (care for the whole person) as we form leaders for and with others.

The difference

What’s different about a business student?

Everyone works with “sports identity,” but the relationship to it differs by population, and that difference is the whole design.

Student-athletes

Transitioning away

Often moving away from a sports identity as a natural part of their journey.

Goal: loosen the fusion and explore who they are beyond sport.

Mature businesses

Sport as credential

Sport is validation, then something to move past.

Goal: hook with sport, then transition to non-sport concepts.

Business students (ours)

Trying to integrate

Underleveraged and compartmentalized, the identity is left at the door.

Goal: surface the sports identity and use it as a bridge to building the future leaders of business, kept central and persistent.

Why business students are a unique opportunity: they want to stay connected to sport while becoming leaders. Sport isn’t a problem to escape or a credential to move past; it’s an underused, authentic vehicle. Less friction, greater upside than the other two demographics.
Where students are today

A real barrier, and an unused lever

The need

Students are experiencing psychological hurdles that stand between them and growing and performing as ethical business leaders.

The unused lever

They carry a valuable sports identity (training, competition, teamwork, performance under pressure) that the business school leaves dormant and compartmentalized.

Those psychological hurdles create a unique barrier to students’ current and future performance, and they already hold an authentic lever, their sports identity, that we’re not using.
The argument for leveraging sports identities
Students are able to allow their sports identities to become salient while exploring the principles, skills, and tools that facilitate their own personal growth, and how those apply to their future performance environments.

I strongly believe we should integrate sport into our training and use it as the tool to reach the shared outcome we’re each passionate about, with sports identity kept as a consistent thread.

The crosswalk

Sport and business are both performance environments

Sport is the native language our students already speak. We build leadership and resilience in that language, then help them carry it into future domains. Core concepts transfer:

Pressure & performance Preparation & routine Mindset / self-talk Teams, roles, trust, vulnerability Resilience & recovery from failure Identity & purpose beyond the scoreboard

Conduit

A vehicle to the Jesuit values and skills we want to build.

Commonality

A shared, understood context, a bridge across a diverse cohort.

Meaningful context

High affinity drives higher engagement, and a higher probability of reaching outcomes.

Three types of potential engagements

Three types of potential engagements in the School of Business

Type 1

Large open sessions

No barriers to engage: raise awareness, start conversations, and drive future engagement at a broader scale.

  • Frameworks & examples to a wide audience, event style
  • Bite-sized video into group discussion (low-resource, sustainable)
  • Open beyond GSCG to the broader business school
  • Content from the campus video team plus interviews of our own athletes
Type 2

Programmatic integration

Build the concepts into an existing program, for example the Gonzaga Sport Consulting Group (GSCG).

  • Implement into GSCG training and events
  • Structured reflection with control over the experience
  • Could become a “GSCG-certified” workshop series
Type 3

Small-group engagements

Content our consultant teams use, where part of a meeting opens discussion and builds a bond.

  • Concepts that spark team discussion within meetings
  • The team bonding we’ve been seeking
  • Smaller groups, team-based development
From here we can build programming for these areas. The GSCG options are something we can go after now; the broader, more systematic programming would raise overall awareness and spark more conversation across the business school, which could feed more opportunities to implement with other programs and groups. We can choose which of these to pursue.
How we build it together

A potential sequence for building an aligned plan for engagement

  1. Shared understanding of the outcome: align on the destination
  2. Shared understanding of where students are today: the starting state
  3. Explore interventions and “treatments”: what raises the probability of reaching the outcomes?
  4. Choose the engagement type(s): large open sessions, programmatic integration (GSCG), or small group
Follow-up

What are your thoughts?

The one thing I feel strongly about: we should continually leverage sports identities to help students move toward the outcomes we both hope for. That consistency, expecting a student’s sports identity to be brought up and used throughout their growth journey, also tells a strong, coherent story to our supporters and audiences.

A working vision for the Gonzaga School of Business and the Extra Mile Institute (EMI). Framing, not a finished program.
